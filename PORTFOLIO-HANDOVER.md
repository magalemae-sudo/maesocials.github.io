## The direction for each concept

| Concept | Proposed goal | Content emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brew & Bean | Help a first-time visitor choose an order and plan a visit. | Menu choices, atmosphere, preparation, and confirmed visit information. |
| Glow Theory | Help people find approved product information and ask specific questions. | Product facts, approved directions, brand identity, and information FAQs. |
| Style Muse | Help shoppers picture a piece and find its details. | Collection information, outfit ideas, accessories, sizing, and ordering questions. |
| Stronger With Sam | Help people understand the confirmed offering and ask informed questions. | Training context, everyday consistency, support expectations, and service FAQs. |

These are distinct proposed approaches, not campaigns that have been run.
Discovery would establish each business’s actual offering, audience, assets,
and capacity. Glow Theory’s product and educational wording requires review;
Stronger With Sam’s service, qualifications, and any instructional content
must be confirmed. Stock imagery does not establish a facility or session.
The fashion and café promotions remain illustrative, with no live offer.

## Evidence to collect when real projects begin

1. **Context:** the business objective, scope, platforms, dates, starting point,
   constraints, and which tasks Mae personally handled.
2. **Starting baseline:** dated native-insight screenshots or exports for reach,
   engagement, followers, profile visits, link clicks, and relevant inquiries.
3. **Comparable follow-up data:** the same metrics, periods, and definitions
   after the work. Keep platform exports rather than relying on recollection.
4. **Execution:** approved calendars, live post links, published screenshots,
   captions, and the approval/revision workflow.
5. **Business outcomes:** qualified inquiries or attributed purchases when the
   business can verify them. Keep the attribution limits visible.
6. **Client feedback:** permission to show the project, business name, and
   testimonial; use the client’s real words and approved identifying details.

Replace a sample with a genuine case study once there is enough evidence.
Explain the objective, work, observed outcome, and limits in the same case.

## Turning visitors into inquiries

- In outreach, share the work most relevant to the business and point to the
  strategy walkthrough when they want to understand Mae’s approach.
- Offer one defined paid first project with agreed deliverables and approval,
  rather than promising growth or making an open-ended offer.
- Clarify platforms, footage/assets, revisions, community work, and reporting
  before quoting. Keep the entry scope useful without underpricing the work.
- Ask what the business does, what is already being posted, and where support
  is needed. The site’s contact helper follows those questions.
